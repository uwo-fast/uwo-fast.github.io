The Free Appropriate Sustainability Technology (FAST) Research Group is led by
Professor [Joshua M. Pearce](https://www.appropedia.org/User:J.M.Pearce), the John M.
Thompson Chair in Information Technology and Innovation at Western University's
[Thompson Centre for Engineering Leadership & Innovation](https://www.eng.uwo.ca/tc/).
Prof. Pearce is also affiliated with the [Ivey Business School](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/)
and the [Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering](https://www.eng.uwo.ca/electrical/).

FAST works on solar photovoltaic technology to power society sustainably, and on
open-source hardware: open-source appropriate technologies (OSAT), and distributed
recycling and additive manufacturing (DRAM) with RepRap-class 3-D printers. The group
contributes to Western's work towards the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals
and its [sustainability goals](https://sustainability.uwo.ca/sdg/index.html).

## Research areas

- **Scientific equipment and instrumentation**: open-source lab equipment that a lab
  can build, repair and modify for a fraction of the commercial cost.
- **Distributed recycling and additive manufacturing**: turning waste plastic into
  feedstock, and printing useful parts locally.
- **Frugal biomedical**: low-cost medical devices that can be made where they are
  needed.
- **Resilient and alternative food**: tools for small farms, and food production that
  keeps working when supply chains do not.
- **Solar photovoltaics**: cheaper, more effective solar power, from open-source
  monitoring to agrivoltaics.

The [projects](/projects/) page lists the group's open-source hardware by area. More of
the group's work, past and present, is on its
[Appropedia page](https://www.appropedia.org/FAST).
